    How tax weeks and tax months work

    What is a tax week?

    The UK tax year starts on 6 April. Tax week 1 is 6 to 12 April, week 2 is 13 to 19 April, and so on in blocks of seven days whatever day of the week 6 April falls on. 52 weeks make 364 days, so one day is left over at the end of the year (5 April), or two days (4 and 5 April) when the tax year contains 29 February. That leftover is week 53.

    What is a tax month?

    Tax month 1 runs from 6 April to 5 May, month 2 from 6 May to 5 June, and so on to month 12, 6 March to 5 April. A date on the 1st to the 5th of a calendar month is in the tax month that started on the 6th of the month before.

    When is PAYE due?

    For monthly payers, PAYE and National Insurance for a tax month must reach HMRC by the 22nd of the next month if you pay electronically, or by the 19th if you post a cheque. So the month ending 5 November is due on 22 November. Small employers who expect to pay less than £1,500 a month on average can pay quarterly instead, by 22 July, 22 October, 22 January and 22 April. When the 22nd is a weekend or bank holiday, the money must arrive by the last working day before, unless you pay by Faster Payments.

    When is the EPS due?

    An Employer Payment Summary, used to reclaim statutory pay, claim Employment Allowance or tell HMRC that you paid no one in a tax month, should reach HMRC by the 19th after the tax month ends (for the month ending 5 November, by 19 November) to change what you owe for that month. The final EPS for the year is due by 19 April.

    Week 53, 54 and 56: what they mean for payroll

    If a weekly payday falls in week 53 (5 April, or 4 or 5 April in a leap year), that year has 53 weekly paydays instead of 52. For fortnightly pay the extra 27th payday is reported as week 54, and for four-weekly pay the extra 14th payday is week 56. HMRC's rule is that the tax on that extra payment is worked out on a non-cumulative basis, using the week 1, week 2 or week 4 free pay. That can leave an employee with a small underpayment at the end of the year. National Insurance is worked out as usual. Monthly payrolls never have a week 53.

    The payday that counts is the contractual payday. If it falls on a weekend or bank holiday and you pay early, HMRC expects you to report the usual payday on the FPS.

    Other payroll year-end dates

    Final FPS on or before the last payday of the year (the final EPS by 19 April), P60s to employees by 31 May, P11D and P11D(b) by 6 July, and Class 1A National Insurance by 22 July if paid electronically (19 July by cheque).

    Does this cover Scotland and Northern Ireland?

    Tax weeks, tax months and the deadlines are the same across the UK. The working-day adjustments here use England and Wales bank holidays. If you bank in Scotland or Northern Ireland, check whether your own bank holidays move a deadline.
