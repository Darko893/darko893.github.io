How the earnings limit works

What counts towards the £204?

Your earnings from a job or self-employment after income tax, National Insurance and allowed expenses. Half of what you pay into a pension is taken off, as are work expenses you need for the job, and paid care costs while you work up to half of your net pay. Occupational and private pensions you receive don't count as earnings.

What if I go over in one week?

There is no sliding scale. If your counted earnings are over £204, you get no Carer's Allowance for that week, £86.45 lost even if you were a penny over. The government asked for views on a taper in 2026, but for now the cliff edge is still the rule. If your pay goes up, tell the Carer's Allowance Unit straight away, because overpayments are claimed back.

I'm paid monthly. How is that turned into weekly?

Monthly pay is multiplied by 12 and divided by 52. Four-weekly pay is divided by 4 and fortnightly pay by 2. If your hours change from week to week, the DWP may look at your earnings over a longer period, so ask them how they'll treat your pay.

What about the old overpayments?

After an independent review, the DWP is looking again at overpayments caused by how it averaged earnings between April 2015 and September 2025. You don't need to apply; it will contact you if it needs more information.

How much is Carer's Allowance?

£86.45 a week in 2026/27. In Scotland, Carer Support Payment has replaced it for new claims, at the same rate and with the same £204 limit.

How is the tax estimate worked out?

For one job on the standard 1257L code: income tax at 20% on weekly pay above £241.73, and National Insurance at 8% on weekly pay above £242. Real payslips can differ, especially in the first weeks of a tax year, with a different code or more than one job, so use your payslip if you have it. Self-employed?
